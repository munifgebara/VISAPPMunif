\section{\uppercase{Kinematic and Pretrained-Feature Comparisons}}
\label{sec:competitive}

\noindent
Experiment~3 asks whether rendering the pen dynamics as an image is worthwhile, by comparing the selected minimaps with two alternatives that each set aside one part of the proposal. The kinematic/pressure SVM represents the conventional use of the same pen signals on PaHaW \cite{Drotar2016}, summarizing speed, acceleration, jerk and pressure as numerical attributes without an image. The ResNet18 pipeline keeps the minimap images but replaces the handcrafted texture descriptor with pretrained convolutional features. Both are re-implemented under the same participant partitions and task voting as the minimaps, so the comparison concerns how the pen signals are represented rather than differences in evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Task Set and Evaluation Protocol}
\noindent
The primary set comprises repeated \textit{l}, \textit{le} and \textit{les} (T2--T4), the tasks examined in the reference thesis~\cite{Casademunt2023}. This set is fixed before fitting; combining its three tasks by voting is our adaptation. Four pipelines are evaluated: the static trace with LPQ and SVM, minimaps with LPQ and SVM, minimaps with frozen ResNet18 features and a trained linear head, and kinematic/pressure attributes with SVM. Both minimap pipelines choose among the nine encodings of Table~\ref{tab:encoding}, including the static trace, by averaging inner-fold scores across the tasks of the set, with ties resolved in favor of the static trace; every choice uses outer-training participants only, and the three SVM pipelines retain the same 21-candidate search.

The primary endpoint is participant-level macro F1 from majority voting over T2--T4 (Section~\ref{sec:fusion}), with no calibration, learned weights or rule selection; recording-level macro F1 over the three tasks is secondary, and all eight tasks are kept as a secondary comparison. Holm adjustment covers the six pipeline pairs separately at each endpoint.

\subsection{Kinematic and Pressure Attributes}
\noindent
The kinematic baseline describes each recording with 83 numbers computed directly from the pen signals, in native tablet units and without an image. Eleven are global, describing the acquisition time and its contact, in-air and transition parts, the number and path length of the contact and in-air strokes, and the size of the written area. The other 72 are the mean, standard deviation, median, 5th and 95th percentiles and interquartile range of each of twelve distributions: stroke duration, stroke length, speed, acceleration and jerk, each for contact and for in-air movement, together with contact pressure and its rate of change. Derivatives are finite differences over the recorded timestamps, with no smoothing and never across a pen lift. The attribute families follow earlier kinematic and pressure studies on PaHaW \cite{Drotar2016}, but the descriptor is fixed before fitting, without selecting attributes by their relation to the labels.

\subsection{Pretrained Features from the Minimaps}
\noindent
ResNet18~\cite{He2016} supplies 512 features from its global average-pooling layer using the fixed TorchVision \texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V1} weights. Images undergo the same foreground crop and square white padding as LPQ, followed by bilinear resizing to $224\times224$ and fixed ImageNet channel normalization; the backbone stays in evaluation mode, with weights and batch-normalization statistics frozen and no augmentation or fine-tuning. A class-balanced, $L_2$-regularized logistic head is fitted to standardized features, its inner search evaluating $C\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,100\}$ and selecting among the same nine encodings as the LPQ pipeline.

\noindent
Minimaps with LPQ and SVM give the best participant-level decisions of the four pipelines (Table~\ref{tab:competitive}), with the highest macro F1 (0.6449), accuracy (64.80\%), balanced accuracy (0.6472), PD sensitivity (0.5892) and H specificity (0.7053). Their margin over the static trace is 0.0307 [$-$0.0107, 0.0748]: encoding the dynamics adds three points of macro F1 with the same descriptor and classifier, although a cohort of 75 participants cannot resolve margins of this size. Training-only selection chose a dynamic encoding in 23 of the 25 training sets with LPQ, most often $PAZ$ (9), and in 24 of 25 with ResNet18, most often $SPZ$ (7).

The kinematic attributes lead the recording-level endpoint (0.6074) and the vote-fraction AUC (0.6691 against 0.6139), so the two pipelines are strongest on different endpoints. An average of per-task scores does not capture how errors coincide across tasks, which is what the participant-level vote rewards.

Voting over all eight tasks instead of T2--T4 gives participant-level macro F1 of 0.6330 for the static trace, 0.6166 for minimaps with LPQ, 0.5941 for kinematic attributes and 0.6225 for minimaps with ResNet18, so the shorter protocol favors the minimap LPQ pipeline, which rises by 0.0283 [$-$0.0358, 0.0950], and the kinematic attributes, which rise by 0.0338. On the spiral, the task furthest from the three selected, minimaps with LPQ give the highest recording-level macro F1, 0.6327 against 0.5846 for the static trace. No ResNet18 policy restricted to the static trace was evaluated, so the results of that pipeline cannot be attributed to the dynamics alone.
