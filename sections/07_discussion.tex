\section{\uppercase{Discussion}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent
The controlled extensions show how the evidence depends on the encoding, task set and classification endpoint. We examine selection within training, spatially aligned signals and competitive models before relating the findings to earlier handwriting studies.

\subsection{Encoding and Spatial Organization}
\noindent
The initial incremental screen found gains on four tasks for $SAZ$, including a 0.0802 increase in spiral macro F1. Its task mean of 0.5752, compared with 0.5643 for static, motivated further evaluation. When encoding selection is confined to training, the nine-encoding procedure achieves 0.5618 and the dynamic-only procedure 0.5643, against 0.5620 for static. None of these paired differences survives Holm correction. These estimates concern adaptive procedures; changes to the inner partitions also distinguish them from the original fixed-$SAZ$ screen.

The encoding choices are unstable: $SAZ$ is selected in four of the 25 outer training sets in the dynamic-only search, and no encoding predominates. With fusion rules selected internally for both policies, nine-encoding selection scores 0.5991 against 0.6171 for static. This compares encoding policies under the same fusion-selection procedure; it does not isolate the benefit of selecting a fusion rule. The experiments provide limited support for a generally preferred dynamic encoding on this cohort.

Aligned dynamics score numerically above circular displacement and joint permutation by 0.0082 and 0.0201 in task-mean macro F1. The latter contrast has a positive pointwise interval but an adjusted $p=0.1848$; neither survives the declared correction. The controls preserve trajectory geometry and joint signal vectors within strokes. Permutation also disrupts local continuity and image texture, so the observed difference cannot identify a physiological mechanism.

\subsection{Task Subsets and Competitive Models}
\noindent
With T2/T3/T4 fixed from the literature and majority voting fixed in advance, the selected-encoding LPQ pipeline achieves participant macro F1 of 0.6449, compared with 0.6143 for static. The paired increase is 0.0307, with a 95\% interval of $[-0.0107,0.0748]$. This is the largest observed primary-endpoint score among the four pipelines, although none of the six primary contrasts survives Holm correction.

The three-task LPQ fusion exceeds its eight-task counterpart by 0.0283, but the interval includes zero. Reducing the task set also helps the kinematic baseline numerically and lowers the static and transfer scores; none of these changes survives correction. Using three tasks reduces the number of exercises; a reduction in total assessment time or clinical burden was not evaluated. Task means over three and eight exercises describe different evaluation scopes.

Model rankings depend on the endpoint. Kinematics has the highest observed three-task mean, whereas ResNet18 transfer leads the eight-task mean at 0.5813 against 0.5618 for selected LPQ, without a supported difference. The earlier result for a CNN trained from scratch offers no basis for a general claim that CNNs are weaker. The transfer experiment evaluates a frozen ImageNet backbone with a trained linear head; architecture, resolution and search budget differ across pipelines.

Our selected LPQ accuracies on T2/T3/T4 individually are 54.67/56.00/63.20\%, below the corresponding thesis values. Its single holdout, crop sampling and crop-size exploration on the designated test group differ from our repeated evaluation. Our three-task fusion is an additional experiment, not a result reported in the thesis.

Drot\'ar et al. combine T2--T8 and report 81.3\% accuracy with kinematic and pressure features~\cite{Drotar2016}. Their validation repeats ten-fold splitting ten times; the description does not fully establish whether statistical feature screening is repeated within training. Diaz et al. report 86.67\% with enhanced images, pretrained features and five selected tasks~\cite{Diaz2019}. Their feature selection is explicitly restricted to training, while the same restriction is not established for task selection.

Our accuracies remain below those published combined-task results. The kinematic baseline retains native signal levels, timing and in-air motion, whereas minimaps normalize size and signal levels and retain contact trajectories. LPQ summarizes local phase without retaining its absolute spatial location. Representation, available information, classifier and evaluation procedure vary together; the comparisons do not isolate the source of the performance gap.

\subsection{Explanation and Generalization}
\noindent
The XAI analysis concerns only the earlier CNN trained from scratch. Its near-zero Grad-CAM--occlusion correlation limits the consistency of the spatial interpretation. Visually plausible maps establish neither disease-specific regions nor an explanation of the transfer model.

All experiments reuse a small cohort that informed development. Participant separation protects each fitted evaluation, while the uncertainty intervals remain conditional on saved predictions and omit the full history of research decisions. Independent-cohort validation is needed to assess transfer across participants, acquisition conditions and demographic distributions.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\noindent
The source package includes stage configurations and code for rendering, LPQ and kinematic extraction, frozen CNN transfer, selection, fusion and XAI. Its execution instructions require the original PaHaW recordings, which are not redistributed. Archived searches, partitions, predictions and checkpoints support independent reconstruction of choices and fusion. Checksums connect code and derived artifacts to their inputs.

\section{\uppercase{Conclusion}}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\noindent
We introduced a new formulation of pseudodynamic handwriting minimap signatures and evaluated its construction incrementally, followed by training-only selection, spatial controls and competitive baselines. The observed responses vary across tasks, with substantial uncertainty about predictive gains. On the literature-defined T2/T3/T4 set, selected LPQ fusion reaches macro F1 of 0.6449 against 0.6143 for static; the paired improvement remains uncertain. Neither the controls nor the competitive comparisons establish overall superiority after multiplicity correction. The formulation and reproducible evaluation provide a basis for testing which aspects of pen dynamics generalize beyond this development cohort.
