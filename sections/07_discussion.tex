\section{\uppercase{Discussion}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent
The controlled extensions show how the evidence depends on the encoding, the task set and the classification endpoint.

\subsection{Encoding and Spatial Organization}
\noindent
The evidence for a dynamic advantage weakens as the selection rule tightens. The initial fixed-encoding screen favoured $SAZ$ by 0.0109 in task mean, with its largest gain on the spiral at 0.0802. Confining the encoding choice to training removes that margin: nine-encoding selection reaches 0.5618 and dynamic-only selection 0.5643, against 0.5620 for static, and the chosen encoding is unstable, with $SAZ$ selected in only four of the 25 outer training sets. Under internally selected fusion the ordering even reverses, 0.5991 against 0.6171 for static. Changes to the inner partitions distinguish these adaptive procedures from the original screen, so the comparison is between policies rather than between fixed representations.

The spatial controls point the same way. Aligned dynamics exceed circular displacement by 0.0082 and joint permutation by 0.0201 in task-mean macro F1; the permutation contrast has a pointwise interval excluding zero but an adjusted $p=0.1848$. Because permutation also disrupts local continuity and image texture, even that margin cannot be attributed to the physiological placement of the signals.

\subsection{Task Subsets and Competitive Models}
\noindent
Rankings depend on the endpoint and on the task set, not only on the representation. With T2/T3/T4 fixed from the literature and majority voting fixed in advance, training-selected minimaps have the highest observed participant macro F1, but the paired interval against static images includes zero. The ordering changes with the endpoint: kinematics lead the three-task mean, and ResNet18 transfer leads the eight-task mean. Reducing the task set raises the selected-encoding and kinematic pipelines numerically while lowering the static and transfer ones. The earlier result for a CNN trained from scratch therefore offers no basis for a general claim that CNNs are weaker.

Shortening the protocol reduces the number of exercises, but we did not evaluate assessment time or clinical burden, and task means over three and eight exercises describe different evaluation scopes. The four pipelines are also unmatched on input resolution and on search budget, not only on the information they receive.

Our selected LPQ accuracies on T2/T3/T4 individually are 54.67/56.00/63.20\%, below the corresponding thesis values. Its single holdout, crop sampling and crop-size exploration on the designated test group differ from our repeated evaluation. Our three-task fusion is an additional experiment, not a result reported in the thesis.

Drot\'ar et al. combine T2--T8 and report 81.3\% accuracy with kinematic and pressure features~\cite{Drotar2016}. Their validation repeats ten-fold splitting ten times; the description does not fully establish whether statistical feature screening is repeated within training. Diaz et al. report 86.67\% with enhanced images, pretrained features and five selected tasks~\cite{Diaz2019}. Their feature selection is explicitly restricted to training, while the same restriction is not established for task selection.

Our accuracies remain below those published combined-task results. The kinematic baseline retains native signal levels, timing and in-air motion, whereas minimaps normalize size and signal levels and retain contact trajectories. LPQ summarizes local phase without retaining its absolute spatial location. Representation, available information, classifier and evaluation procedure vary together; the comparisons do not isolate the source of the performance gap.

\subsection{Explanation and Generalization}
\noindent
A separate case study applied Grad-CAM~\cite{Selvaraju2017} and patch occlusion to the frozen outer-fold checkpoints of the scratch-trained CNN, explaining each image with the checkpoint that produced its held-out prediction. Attribution concentrates on the writing. Participant-averaged foreground enrichment is 1.803 [1.723, 1.884]. Whitening the 10\% of pixels with the greatest Grad-CAM values costs 0.0489 [0.0427, 0.0553] of predicted-class confidence, against 0.0033 [0.0016, 0.0050] for an equal-area random deletion. The two attribution methods nevertheless disagree spatially, with a median pixelwise Spearman correlation of $-$0.0121. Concentration on handwriting therefore identifies no disease-specific image feature, and these explanations describe that small CNN alone, not the transfer pipeline.

All experiments reuse a small cohort that informed development. Participant separation protects each fitted evaluation, while the uncertainty intervals remain conditional on saved predictions and omit the full history of research decisions. Independent-cohort validation is needed to assess transfer across participants, acquisition conditions and demographic distributions.

A source package provides stage configurations and code for rendering, LPQ and kinematic extraction, frozen CNN transfer, selection, fusion and attribution, with checksums linking code and derived artifacts to their inputs. Archived searches, partitions, predictions and checkpoints allow the choices and the fusion to be reconstructed independently; running the stages requires the original PaHaW recordings, which we do not redistribute.

\section{\uppercase{Conclusion}}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\noindent
We introduced pseudodynamic minimaps and evaluated their construction incrementally, followed by training-only selection, spatial controls and competitive baselines. Encoding pen dynamics as colour changes task-level behaviour, but on this cohort the change is not large enough to separate the representations once the encoding is chosen inside training. Which pipeline leads depends on the task set and on the endpoint rather than on the representation alone, and that dependence is the result most likely to transfer. The formulation and its reproducible evaluation provide a basis for testing which aspects of pen dynamics survive beyond this development cohort.
