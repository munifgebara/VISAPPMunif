\keywords{Handwriting, Pseudodynamic Images, Local Phase Quantization, Parkinson's Disease, Explainable AI.}
\abstract{Pseudodynamic handwriting minimap signatures preserve the visible pen trajectory while assigning recorded movement signals to colour channels. We investigate whether this compact representation benefits Parkinson's disease classification and whether increasing classifier complexity improves its use. On 597 PaHaW recordings from 75 participants across eight tasks, we compare a static baseline, four isolated signals (speed, pressure, altitude and azimuth) and four RGB combinations using Local Phase Quantization (LPQ) and SVM, with repeated nested model fitting in common participant-separated partitions. Speed, altitude and azimuth rank first, with task-mean macro F1 of 0.5752 versus 0.5643 for static, but no encoding difference survives multiplicity correction. In an exploratory comparison using this SVM-selected encoding, LPQ-based classifiers exceed a small CNN trained from scratch; SVM reaches 0.5752 versus 0.5105, with Holm-adjusted $p=0.0054$. Majority voting across tasks yields participant-level macro F1 of 0.6498 for logistic regression and 0.6488 for SVM, a distinct endpoint from the task mean. Held-out Grad-CAM concentrates on writing, yet its median correlation with occlusion is $-0.0121$. These findings support fixed texture features as a competitive baseline in this cohort and show why adding recorded signals and interpreting attribution maps each require empirical validation.}
