\section{\uppercase{Discussion}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent
The three experiments indicate that the classifiers use the pen dynamics encoded in the minimaps. Training-only selection chose a dynamic encoding in 24 of 25 training sets in Experiment~2 even though ties favored the static trace, and displacing the signals within strokes lowered recording-level macro F1, most under joint permutation, so the position of each value along the trajectory matters and not only the colors present.

The gains depend on the task (Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}). One encoding chosen for all eight tasks averages gains on the spiral, T6 and T8 with losses on T2, T3, T5 and T7, which keeps its eight-task mean close to the static trace, whereas on the homogeneous letter patterns of T2--T4 it gives the best participant-level decisions. Choosing encodings per task is the direct way to exploit this dependence.

The pipelines are also strong on different endpoints rather than uniformly: minimaps with LPQ give the best participant-level decisions, while the kinematic attributes lead the recording-level mean and the vote-fraction AUC. The representation works with both a handcrafted texture descriptor and pretrained convolutional features, and this complementarity between the image and kinematic pipelines motivates combining them.

These effects are small, and their size is not established. The margin of the minimap pipeline over the static trace on T2--T4 is 0.0307 [$-$0.0107, 0.0748], an interval that includes zero; no contrast between encodings in Experiment~1 survives multiplicity correction; and the one effect whose interval excludes zero, the joint-permutation control, reaches adjusted $p=0.1848$ within its family. A cohort of 75 participants cannot resolve differences of this order.

Three limitations bound the representation itself. The image keeps contact trajectories only, so in-air movement and the duration of pen lifts are absent. Fitting every record to its own canvas removes absolute writing size, and with it micrographia. And no ResNet18 policy restricted to the static trace was evaluated, so the behavior of that pipeline cannot be separated from the encoding. Each is a direct extension rather than an obstacle.

Published results on PaHaW are higher than ours, and the protocols differ enough that the gap is not a ranking. Drot\'ar et al. report 81.30\% accuracy from kinematic and pressure features over T2--T8 under repeated ten-fold cross-validation \cite{Drotar2016}, and Diaz et al. 86.67\% with enhanced images, pretrained features and an ensemble of the five tasks with the highest measured accuracy \cite{Diaz2019}; the thesis that fixes our task set reports 69.23, 61.54 and 69.23\% on T2, T3 and T4 from a single split of 62 training and 13 test participants \cite{Casademunt2023}. Our 64.80\% is a majority vote over the three tasks with every choice made inside training, on a cohort where one participant is worth 1.3 points of accuracy. Input, task set, selection procedure and evaluation scope vary together across these studies, so the numbers describe different experiments rather than competing methods.

\section{\uppercase{Conclusion}}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\noindent
This initial study introduced pseudodynamic minimaps, a new image representation that places pen speed, pressure, altitude and azimuth as color along the handwriting trajectory. Evaluated on PaHaW with participant-separated partitions, the representation was preferred to static traces by a training-based selection procedure, displacing the dynamics within each stroke reduced its performance, and on the three repeated-pattern tasks it gave the best participant-level results of the four pipelines compared. These findings are consistent in direction but small in magnitude, and none of them separates the representations once multiplicity is accounted for on a cohort of 75 participants. We therefore offer pseudodynamic minimaps as an explicit way to carry pen dynamics into image-based handwriting analysis, and as a formulation to be tested further, rather than as a demonstrated improvement.

Future work will choose encodings per task, combine minimaps with kinematic descriptors, fine-tune pretrained networks on the minimaps, and re-implement the closest image encodings under this protocol, which is the comparison that would establish whether the formulation improves on them. Independent cohorts are needed to assess how any of this transfers.
