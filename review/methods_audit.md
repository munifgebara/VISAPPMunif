# Methods audit for the rewritten VISAPP paper

Audited against the executed experiment configurations, Python implementation, and saved predictions on 2026-09-08. This is a factual methods sheet and an editorial review; it does not change code, rerun models, or revise recorded results. The stroke-width experiment is outside the manuscript scope requested by the author and should not appear in its narrative, tables, figures, or supplements.

## Findings that affect the central claims

1. **Representation selection was exploratory.** SAZ was selected as the highest-ranked encoding using the same outer-test predictions later used to report its score. Nested validation isolates classifier fitting and tuning, but does not provide an independent test of the full process that selected SAZ. The later algorithm comparison is conditional on an encoding chosen with SVM on this cohort. Describe it as development on PaHaW, not untouched external validation or unbiased evaluation of a selected end-to-end pipeline. Source: `experiments/2026-09-restart/encoding-comparison-v1/config.json`, `selection_rule`; `classical-classifier-comparison-v1/config.json`, `selected_encoding` and SVM prediction reuse.

2. **Uncertainty is conditional on saved predictions.** Bootstrap samples and paired randomization swaps operate on participant-indexed prediction vectors. They preserve the repeated observations and tasks of one participant together, which is appropriate for that dependency, but do not retrain models, redo feature/representation selection, or reproduce training-set uncertainty. Training folds also overlap. Present the intervals and p-values as conditional, exploratory comparisons of this run, without claiming confirmatory generalization significance. Sources: `src/pdhms_restart/comparison.py:246` and `src/pdhms_restart/evaluation.py:269`.

3. **The CNN did not use three-fold epoch selection.** Classical models used three inner folds. CNN epochs were selected on the first frozen inner split only and then a newly initialized network was fitted to all outer-training participants. The architecture and optimizer were fixed. Source: `scripts/run_cnn.py:353`.

4. **Azimuth was centered circularly, not temporally unwrapped.** The single-signal report's wording “unwrapped circularly” does not match the implementation. The code subtracts a recording-level circular mean, wraps to a principal angular interval, and averages adjacent centered values arithmetically. Source: `src/pdhms_restart/dynamic_image.py:78`.

5. **XAI evidence is mixed and its valid sample denominators matter.** The median Grad-CAM–occlusion correlation uses 540 nonconstant pairs out of 597; the trained–random correlation uses 160 nonconstant pairs out of 200. Top-10% IoU includes degenerate maps, for which top-pixel selection is arbitrary. Equal-area random deletion is a limited perturbation control, not a definitive validation of localization. Sources: `scripts/run_cnn_xai.py:91`, `src/pdhms_restart/xai.py:78` and `:177`; counts independently recomputed from the saved CSV files.

## Dataset and endpoints

There are 75 participants, 38 healthy controls and 37 with Parkinson's disease, with 597 available recordings. Task 1 has 72 recordings, 36 per diagnosis; tasks 2–8 each have 75. Each filename has repetition index 1. Repeated letters or words within a recording are part of that task, not additional independent samples. The absence of three spiral recordings is not a quality-based exclusion established by this audit. The task labels are: Archimedean spiral; repeated `l`; repeated `le`; repeated `les`; `lektorka`; `porovnat`; `nepopadnout`; and `Tramvaj dnes už nepojede.` Source: `experiments/2026-09-restart/CONTEXT.md:45`.

Use “handwriting and drawing tasks” when first introducing all eight tasks, followed by “tasks.” The unit of separation and resampling is the participant, including controls; “patient-independent” alone is less precise.

The reader loads columns as `y, x, timestamp, state, azimuth, altitude, pressure`; state 1 indicates surface contact. Metadata are read from the supplied workbook. Acquisition is documented locally as Wacom Intuos 4M, 150 Hz. Five files have declared point counts different from the number of rows; the loader uses all observed numeric rows. No files have non-finite values, invalid contact state, or no drawable surface segments. There are 253 negative timestamp steps across 151 records, but all 1,065,788 consecutive on-surface segments have positive time differences; no speed is imputed. Sources: `src/pdhms_restart/data.py:15`, `:111`, `:123`; `runs/static-svm-v1/reports/static_svm_report.md:32`; `runs/dynamic-single-signal-v1/reports/dynamic_single_signal_report.md:7`.

The local workbook audit records mean age 62.42 years (SD 11.39) for controls versus 69.32 (SD 10.97) for PD. Age is not an image-model input, but this imbalance remains a possible confound and has not been adjusted away. Source: `experiments/2026-09-restart/CONTEXT.md:66`.

Task macro-F1 is computed by pooling all held-out participants within one repeat, then averaging the five repeat scores. The global task endpoint is the unweighted average across the eight task scores and five repeats, not macro-F1 from concatenating all recordings and not the mean of 25 fold-level F1 values. Sources: `evaluation.py:269`; `comparison.py:273`.

## From static geometry to dynamic colour

The static image is a reconstruction of the online trajectory, not a scanned offline image. Only pairs of consecutive samples that both have contact state 1 are connected. Pen-up travel is omitted, including in bounding-box estimation. Each recording is translated, centered, and uniformly scaled to a 512 × 512 white canvas with a 20-pixel margin, preserving aspect ratio. Lines use RGB (30,30,30) and fixed width 4 pixels. Rendering is performed at three times the resolution and reduced with Lanczos. There is no physical-scale preservation, absolute-size feature, duration feature, or elapsed-time colour. Sources: `src/pdhms_restart/static_image.py:15`, `:26`, `:55`.

For each eligible segment, speed is Euclidean displacement in the original coordinate columns divided by its positive timestamp difference, followed by `log1p`. No conversion to millimetres or seconds occurs; report the formula in acquisition units, not a physical speed unit that the code did not compute. Pressure and altitude are arithmetic means of the two segment endpoints. For azimuth, the raw values are interpreted as tenths of a degree, converted to radians, centered around the circular mean of on-surface samples, wrapped through the complex argument, converted back to tenths of a degree, and then endpoint-averaged. Altitude and azimuth describe stylus orientation, not the direction of the writing trajectory. Source: `src/pdhms_restart/dynamic_image.py:50`.

Each signal is normalized independently within its own recording: subtract its fifth percentile over eligible segments, divide by the 95th–5th percentile range, and clip to [0,1]. Degenerate ranges map to 0.5, although none occurred in the isolated-signal execution. Intensity is rounded from `30 + 190 × normalized_value`. This is a deterministic, label-free, per-recording transform usable at inference; it is not a training-population normalization or calibrated physical colour scale. It preserves relative within-recording variation while suppressing absolute level and range differences. Spatial fitting also removes absolute writing size. These are material representation limits. Source: `dynamic_image.py:94`.

The four isolated variants encode speed, pressure, altitude, or azimuth in red while holding green and blue at 30. The four triplets are SPA, SPZ, SAZ, and PAZ, with signals assigned in the stated order to red, green, and blue. Every variant uses the same geometry, contact mask, canvas, line width, and antialiasing. A segment receives a constant colour; later segments overwrite earlier ones at overlaps. SAZ means red speed, green altitude, blue azimuth. Sources: `dynamic_image.py:115`, `:180`; `dynamic-triplet-svm-v1/config.json`.

No channel-order search was performed. For the standardized linear/RBF SVM and concatenated per-channel LPQ, an RGB channel permutation is a permutation of descriptor blocks, preserving dot products and Euclidean distances; this justified avoiding redundant SVM permutations. Do not extend this exact invariance claim to a fixed trained CNN or Random Forest.

## Texture descriptor and classical classifiers

All classical models use LPQ-RGB. The image is foreground-cropped using any channel below 250, expanded by a margin equal to 5% of the larger bounding-box side, padded centrally to a white square, and bilinearly resized to 64 × 64. Each channel is processed separately with four complex Fourier filters on a 5 × 5 window, corresponding to frequency directions (0,1), (1,0), (1,1), and (1,−1), with frequency magnitude 1/5 in pixel units. Reflect boundary handling is used. Signs of real and imaginary responses produce eight binary bits. A 256-bin normalized histogram is square-root transformed per channel and the three histograms are concatenated into 768 features. No covariance decorrelation, spatial pyramid, learned feature selection, or auxiliary geometric feature is implemented. The static image's three channel blocks are identical; 768 is its stored dimension, not 768 independent features. Source: `src/pdhms_restart/lpq.py:25`, `:52`, `:74`.

The common split file is generated by stratified splitting of one row per participant: five independent repetitions of five outer folds. Participant assignments are propagated to all their tasks. Three stratified inner folds are generated inside each task's available outer-training participants. Thus held-out participants contribute no recordings of any task to the model's training set. All representations and methods reuse the saved assignments. Each task has a separate fitted classifier. Source: `src/pdhms_restart/evaluation.py:65`.

SVM uses a pipeline of StandardScaler and class-balanced SVC. Search comprises five linear candidates with C in {0.01,0.1,1,10,100}, and sixteen RBF candidates with C in {0.1,1,10,100} and gamma in {scale,0.0001,0.001,0.01}. Mean inner macro-F1 chooses the candidate; ties retain the first declared candidate. Scaling is fitted anew within each training split, and the selected pipeline is refitted to all outer-training records for that task. Source: `evaluation.py:39`, `:51`, `:159`; `static-svm-v1/config.json`.

Logistic regression uses StandardScaler, balanced class weights, liblinear, L1 or L2 regularization and C in {0.001,0.01,0.1,1,10,100}; maximum iterations are 10,000. Random Forest uses 100 bootstrap trees, Gini impurity, balanced-subsample class weights, max_features in {sqrt,0.25}, and minimum leaf size in {1,2,4}; no explicit depth bound or feature scaling is applied. Both use the same three-fold inner macro-F1 selection followed by full outer-training fitting. Sources: `src/pdhms_restart/classical_models.py:29`, `:67`, `:111`; `classical-classifier-comparison-v1/config.json`.

## CNN progression

The CNN consumes cropped, square-padded, bilinearly resized 128 × 128 SAZ pixels and a fixed transformation from [0,255] to [−1,1]. It is trained from scratch with no pretrained weights. Four blocks each contain a bias-free padded 3 × 3 convolution, batch normalization, ReLU, and 2 × 2 max pooling. Channel counts are 16,32,64,128. The resulting 128 × 8 × 8 map is flattened, followed by dropout 0.3 and a single linear logit. The total is 105,873 trainable parameters. Source: `src/pdhms_restart/cnn_model.py:15`.

AdamW uses learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.0001, and batch size 16. Weighted binary cross-entropy sets positive-class weight to the negative/positive training count, computed separately for the inner selection fit and final outer fit. Training-only random affine augmentation uses rotations up to 3°, translations up to 3% on each axis, and scale [0.95,1.05], with white fill and no flips. The fixed decision threshold is sigmoid output ≥0.5 for PD. Sources: `scripts/run_cnn.py:43`, `:152`, `:209`; `cnn-v1/config.json`.

For each of the 200 task/repeat/outer-fold models, epochs are selected using only the first frozen inner validation fold, maximizing macro-F1 and breaking ties by lower validation BCE. Selection runs for at most 60 epochs, may stop after at least 12 epochs once 10 epochs without improvement accrue, and may choose an epoch before 12. A newly initialized CNN is then trained on all outer-training participants for the selected epoch count. Observed selected epochs span 1–48, with median 15. No architecture search or repeated neural initialization comparison was performed. Source: `scripts/run_cnn.py:247`, `:285`, `:353`; `runs/cnn-v1/metrics/selected_epochs.csv`.

## Fusion and inference

The common four-model fusion comparison averages hard predictions across all available tasks and declares PD when the fraction is at least 0.5. In eight-task ties (four votes each), PD is therefore selected. Missing spirals are handled by averaging the remaining seven tasks. This creates one held-out prediction per participant per repeat, 375 repeated predictions for 75 people. Majority fusion is a secondary participant endpoint, distinct from the mean of task-level macro-F1. Source: `scripts/run_cnn.py:508`.

A preceding SVM-only fusion experiment declared the unweighted mean of internally sigmoid-calibrated task probabilities as primary and evaluated majority and reliability-weighted fusion as alternatives. Calibration uses three frozen inner folds with CalibratedClassifierCV, ensemble=True, and the selected SVM candidate. Reliability weights are max(inner macro-F1−0.5,0.01). The primary calibrated-mean score must not be silently reclassified as a prespecified majority endpoint; the later cross-classifier comparison explicitly chose majority. Sources: `task-fusion-v1/config.json`; `scripts/run_task_fusion.py:100`. If all fusion rules are not discussed in the main paper, report the SVM alternatives in the figure-selection supplement or associated reproducibility notes.

Bootstrap CIs use 10,000 resamples stratified by diagnosis, sampling participants with replacement and carrying all their task/repeat predictions together. For comparisons, method-label swaps are drawn at participant level and shared across tasks/repeats, with two-sided Monte Carlo p=(1+extreme swaps)/(10,001). Holm adjustment is within declared comparison families: 36 global pairs across nine encodings; six global pairs across four classifiers at the task endpoint; six pairs at fused participant endpoint. Taskwise families are separate. Intervals are 2.5th/97.5th percentiles and are not multiplicity-adjusted simultaneous intervals. Sources: `src/pdhms_restart/comparison.py:43`, `:114`, `:246`, `:344`; experiment configs.

## XAI implementation and interpretation

Each explanation uses the saved outer-fold model that produced the corresponding held-out prediction. Grad-CAM covers all 2,985 repeated predictions. Its target is the originally predicted class: positive logit for PD, negative logit for H. At the last ReLU before the fourth max pool (a 128-channel, 16 × 16 activation map), channel weights are spatial means of the target gradients; weighted activations are summed, rectified, bilinearly resized to 128 × 128, and divided by the map's positive maximum. All-zero maps remain zero. Source: `src/pdhms_restart/xai.py:17`; `cnn_model.py:39`.

Targeted deletion whitens the top 10% of all image pixels by Grad-CAM, across all RGB channels. It is compared with one deterministic equal-area random pixel mask per prediction. Drops refer to confidence in the original prediction, with no requirement that it be correct. Channel perturbation whitens one entire RGB channel. Occlusion uses nonoverlapping 16 × 16 white patches (stride 16, 64 patches per image), clips confidence increases to zero, and rescales positive drops to a per-image maximum. It covers all 597 records in repeat 1. Source: `xai.py:57`, `:78`, `:127`.

The random-weight control uses one deterministically selected held-out image per saved checkpoint (the first participant in that checkpoint's sorted test rows), creates a fully reinitialized model, and explains the original trained model's target class. It is not a layer-by-layer randomization study or label randomization. There are 200 checks, not 200 independent subjects. Source: `scripts/run_cnn_xai.py:249`.

Foreground enrichment is the fraction of positive attribution on rendered foreground divided by the foreground fraction of image area. It measures concentration relative to uniform spatial attribution, not a calibrated chance probability. The reported 1.8026 is a participant mean after averaging repeats within task, then available tasks; zero maps yield missing enrichment and are omitted from means. Its CI [1.7234,1.8842] describes that aggregation. Source: `scripts/run_cnn_xai.py:83`, `:97`, `:289`.

Saved CSVs show 174/2,985 degenerate Grad-CAM maps and no degenerate occlusion maps. Grad-CAM–occlusion median Spearman is −0.012108 over 540 defined pairs out of 597. Trained–random median Spearman is 0.027625 over 160 defined pairs out of 200; 33 random maps are zero. The reported mean top-10% IoUs (0.103620 and 0.112534) include all pairs, including zero maps with arbitrary top-pixel membership, so these figures require that qualification or can be omitted. Sources: `runs/cnn-xai-v1/metrics/occlusion_metrics.csv`, `weight_randomization_sanity.csv`, `prediction_xai_metrics.csv`; `scripts/run_cnn_xai.py:91`.

The targeted-minus-random confidence drop is 0.0456, CI [0.0395,0.0518]. This supports sensitivity to the chosen perturbation, but random masks are not matched for foreground pixels, contiguity, or altered colour distribution; most random deletions in a sparse handwriting image hit white background. Deletion and whitening can also create inputs outside the training distribution. Avoid “passed fidelity validation.” Whole-channel drops of 0.0429 (speed), 0.0482 (altitude), and 0.0427 (azimuth) do not establish physiological importance or rank signals causally, because removing a channel also changes colour and geometry contrast.

Example figures for task 4 select median-confidence cases within each true-diagnosis/correctness stratum in repeat 1. They are diagnostic examples, not randomly sampled proof of localization. Mean maps combine images aligned only by cropping/scaling; they should not be interpreted as corresponding anatomical or stroke positions. Source: `scripts/run_cnn_xai.py:323`, `:396`.

## Edits recommended when turning the package into prose

- Replace “effectively tied” or “equivalent” with “nearly identical point estimates, with no detected difference.” No equivalence margin or equivalence test was specified.
- Replace “all classical methods outperformed the CNN” with “the tested CNN had lower scores than all three classical baselines; paired conditional contrasts remained below 0.05 after Holm correction.” State the tested architecture and developmental setting in the surrounding paragraph.
- Replace “fusion improved performance” with the concrete participant-level fused score and explicitly distinguish it from the task-mean endpoint; the two metrics summarize different predictions.
- Replace “SAZ is the best coding” with “SAZ ranked first numerically and was carried forward for exploratory comparisons.” SAZ–static is +0.0109, 95% CI [−0.0146,0.0370], Holm p=1; SAZ–PAZ is +0.0006, CI [−0.0185,0.0198], Holm p=1.
- Do not claim speed replaces pressure without loss as an established result: the point estimates are close, but non-significance is not a noninferiority test.
- Avoid “XAI explains the CNN's failure.” The analyses describe its attribution and perturbation behaviour; they do not identify why its predictive score was lower.
- Say “held-out outer-fold” rather than “external validation,” to avoid implying a separate institution or unseen cohort.
- Exclude the author-rejected stroke-width experiment throughout the article and alternatives. State fixed rendering width as a method parameter without a negative-result justification.
