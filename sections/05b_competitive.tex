\section{\uppercase{Kinematic and Pretrained-Feature Comparisons}}
\label{sec:competitive}

\noindent
Experiment~3 asks whether rendering the pen dynamics as an image is worthwhile, by comparing the selected minimaps with two alternatives that each set aside one part of the proposal. The kinematic/pressure SVM represents the conventional use of the same pen signals on PaHaW \cite{Drotar2016}: it summarizes speed, acceleration, jerk and pressure as numerical attributes, without an image. The ResNet18 pipeline keeps the minimap images but replaces the handcrafted texture descriptor with pretrained convolutional features. Both are re-implemented under the same participant partitions and task voting as the minimaps, so the comparison concerns how the pen signals are represented rather than differences in evaluation protocol. The experiment uses a task set drawn from related work, which also shortens the handwriting protocol, and keeps all eight tasks as a secondary comparison.

\subsection{Task Set and Evaluation Protocol}
\noindent
The primary set comprises repeated \textit{l}, \textit{le} and \textit{les} (T2--T4), the tasks examined in the reference thesis~\cite{Casademunt2023}. We fix this set before fitting the models, and combining its three tasks by voting is our adaptation. Four pipelines are evaluated: the static trace with LPQ and SVM, minimaps with LPQ and SVM, minimaps with frozen ResNet18 features and a trained linear head, and kinematic/pressure attributes with SVM. Both minimap pipelines choose among the nine encodings of Table~\ref{tab:encoding}, including the static trace.

The pipelines share the 25 outer participant partitions and three common inner folds. For each task and eligible encoding, mean inner-fold macro F1 selects the classifier parameters, and each minimap pipeline chooses one encoding for the task set by averaging these scores across its tasks, with ties resolved in favor of the static trace. Every choice uses only outer-training participants; standardization is fitted within the training partition, and selected models are refitted on the complete outer-training set. The three SVM pipelines retain the same 21-candidate linear/RBF search.

The primary endpoint is participant-level macro F1 from majority voting over T2--T4 (Section~\ref{sec:fusion}), with no calibration, learned weights or rule selection. Recording-level macro F1 over the three tasks is secondary. Holm adjustment covers the six pipeline pairs separately at each endpoint, and the four within-pipeline contrasts between three-task and eight-task voting form another family.

\subsection{Kinematic and Pressure Attributes}
\noindent
The kinematic baseline describes each recording with 83 numbers computed directly from the pen signals, in native tablet units and without an image. Eleven are global: the acquisition time and its contact, in-air and transition parts, the fraction of time in contact, the numbers of contact and in-air strokes, their path lengths, and the width and height of the written area. The other 72 summarize twelve distributions: stroke duration, stroke length, speed, acceleration and jerk, each for contact and for in-air movement, together with contact pressure and its rate of change. Each distribution contributes its mean, standard deviation, median, 5th and 95th percentiles and interquartile range. The attribute families follow earlier kinematic and pressure studies on PaHaW \cite{Drotar2016}, but the descriptor is specified independently and fixed before fitting, without selecting attributes by their relation to the labels.

Speed, acceleration and jerk are finite differences of pen position over the recorded timestamps, converted from milliseconds to seconds, with no smoothing and never across a pen lift. A label-free audit excluded 255 of the 1,364,022 intervals, those with a negative time step or a jump longer than 60~s, none of them during contact; pauses of up to 60~s are kept. When a recording has no in-air movement, or too few samples for a derivative, the corresponding summaries are set to zero, so every recording keeps all 83 attributes.

\subsection{Pretrained Features from the Minimaps}
\noindent
ResNet18~\cite{He2016} supplies 512 features from its global average-pooling layer using the fixed TorchVision \texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V1} weights. Images undergo the same foreground crop and square white padding as LPQ, followed by bilinear resizing to $224\times224$ and fixed ImageNet channel normalization. The backbone remains in evaluation mode, with convolutional weights and batch-normalization statistics frozen. There is no augmentation or end-to-end fine-tuning.

A class-balanced, $L_2$-regularized logistic head is fitted to standardized features. Its inner search evaluates seven values, $C\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,100\}$, and selects among the same nine encodings as the LPQ pipeline. The comparison therefore concerns complete learning procedures with different feature families and search budgets.

\noindent
Minimaps with LPQ and SVM give the best participant-level decisions of the four pipelines (Table~\ref{tab:competitive}). They have the highest macro F1 (0.6449), accuracy (64.80\%), balanced accuracy (0.6472), PD sensitivity (0.5892) and H specificity (0.7053). Their margin over the static trace is 0.0307 [$-$0.0107, 0.0748]: encoding the dynamics adds three points of macro F1 with the same descriptor and classifier, although a cohort of 75 participants cannot resolve margins of this size. Training-only selection chose a dynamic encoding in 23 of the 25 training sets with LPQ, most often $PAZ$ (9), and in 24 of 25 with ResNet18, most often $SPZ$ (7).

The kinematic attributes lead the recording-level endpoint (0.6074) and the vote-fraction AUC (0.6691 against 0.6139), so the two pipelines are strongest on different endpoints. An average of per-task scores does not capture how errors coincide across tasks, which is what the participant-level vote rewards.

\subsection{Effect of the Task Subset}
\noindent
With all eight tasks, majority voting reaches participant-level macro F1 of 0.6330 for the static trace with LPQ, 0.6166 for minimaps with LPQ, 0.5941 for kinematic attributes and 0.6225 for minimaps with ResNet18. Experiment~3 always votes, whereas Experiment~2 chose the rule within training, so the participant-level scores of the two experiments are not directly comparable.

The shorter protocol suits the minimap LPQ pipeline. Moving from eight tasks to T2--T4 raises its participant-level macro F1 by 0.0283 [$-$0.0358, 0.0950] and that of the kinematic attributes by 0.0338, while the static trace changes by $-$0.0188 and minimaps with ResNet18 by $-$0.0204. Recording-level means over three and eight tasks describe different task populations and are not compared directly.

The eight tasks also show that the pipelines are complementary (Table~\ref{tab:per-task}). Minimaps with LPQ give the highest recording-level macro F1 on the spiral, 0.6327 against 0.5846 for the static trace, 0.5984 with ResNet18 and 0.5074 for kinematic attributes, whereas kinematic attributes lead T2, T3 and T8 and minimaps with ResNet18 lead T4--T7. Minimaps with ResNet18 also have the highest recording-level mean over the eight tasks (0.5813, against 0.5618 with LPQ), so pretrained CNN features are a competitive alternative extractor for the same images. No ResNet18 policy restricted to the static trace was evaluated, so this advantage cannot be attributed to the dynamics alone.
