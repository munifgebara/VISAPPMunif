\section{\uppercase{Data and Evaluation}}
\label{sec:protocol}

\noindent
Each recording produces nine alternative images, all derived from the same participant. We compare encodings on common participant partitions and define separate endpoints for individual tasks and decisions combined across tasks.

\subsection{Participants and Tasks}
\noindent
PaHaW provides eight tasks recorded with a Wacom Intuos 4M tablet \cite{Drotar2016}. We calculate speed from the recorded timestamps rather than an assumed sampling interval. Our local inventory contains 597 recordings from 75 participants: 37 with PD and 38 controls. Table~\ref{tab:tasks} specifies the task set. Three spiral recordings are absent from the local corpus; the other tasks have one recording for every participant. We retain all available records and do not interpret missing files as quality exclusions.

An input audit checked file counts, sample counts, finite values and timestamp increments. Five files declare a sample count different from the rows present, and the renderer uses the rows available. Of the 253 negative timestamp increments, none falls inside a contact segment: all 1,065,788 segments with contact at both endpoints increase in time, so Equation~\ref{eq:speed} requires no interpolation. No normalization range was degenerate. The kinematic baseline additionally uses in-air intervals and states its timestamp rules in Section~\ref{sec:competitive}.

The participant is the independent observational unit; tasks and alternative renderings do not enlarge the cohort. Mean age is 69.32 years in PD and 62.42 in H. Age and clinical fields are excluded from classifier inputs, but handwriting may still carry this confounding.

\subsection{A Common Partition for Every Task}
\noindent
We construct stratified folds from one row per participant, then propagate the assignments to every task and rendering. Five repetitions of five outer folds produce a test prediction for every available record in each repetition. All tasks from a participant are in the same outer fold. The spiral task uses the participants for whom a record exists.

Within each outer training partition, three participant-separated inner folds choose the classical model hyperparameters. Feature standardization is fitted on the corresponding training portion, never on its validation or test participants. The selected model is refitted on all outer training participants and evaluated once on the outer test set. The rendering is independent per record, so it has no parameters learned from the other records before splitting.

In the initial eight-task evaluation, each fixed encoding and classifier yields 200 outer models and 2,985 outer-test predictions. These are repeated predictions of 597 records from 75 participants. Every later stage reuses the saved outer assignments. The follow-up stages use three inner folds common across tasks, intersected with available recordings.

The initial highest-ranked encoding was selected from outer scores for the first classifier comparison and XAI analysis. That stage protects hyperparameter fitting but not representation choice. Sections~\ref{sec:nested-followup} and~\ref{sec:competitive} subsequently evaluate encoding selection within training. They estimate different learning procedures and retain the limitation that earlier exploration used the same cohort.

\subsection{Metrics and Paired Comparisons}
\noindent
Macro F1 gives equal weight to the two diagnosis classes:
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{macro}}=\frac{F_{1,\mathrm{H}}+F_{1,\mathrm{PD}}}{2}.
\end{equation}
For each task and repetition, we concatenate the five outer-test folds and compute the metric once. We then average the five resulting scores. For the initial representation and classifier rankings, the endpoint is the unweighted mean across all eight tasks:
\begin{equation}
 \overline F_{\mathrm{tasks}}=\frac{1}{8\cdot5}\sum_{t=1}^{8}\sum_{r=1}^{5}F_{t,r}.
 \label{eq:taskmetric}
\end{equation}
We also retain accuracy, balanced accuracy, PD sensitivity, H specificity, positive-class F1 and ROC AUC.

\subsection{One Decision per Participant}
\label{sec:fusion}
\noindent
Late fusion combines the available task predictions into a single decision per participant. Because all tasks share the participant folds, every model contributing to a held-out person's decision was fitted without that person. We consider three rules: the mean calibrated PD probability, a reliability-weighted mean with weight $\max(F_{\mathrm{inner}}-0.5,0.01)$ derived from inner-validation macro F1, and majority voting. If $\mathcal{T}_j$ is the set of tasks available for participant $j$, its vote fraction in repetition $r$ is
\begin{equation}
 s_{j,r}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_j|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_j}\widehat y_{j,t,r},
 \qquad \widehat y_{j,r}=\mathbf{1}[s_{j,r}\geq0.5].
\end{equation}
Tied votes are assigned to PD by this fixed threshold, and a missing spiral is omitted from that participant's denominator. We compute the participant-level metric separately for each repetition and average the five scores, without averaging probabilities across repetitions. On the initial fixed-encoding SVM the three rules reach macro F1 0.6341, 0.6447 and 0.6488, and no pairwise contrast survives its Holm family. We therefore fix majority voting in advance wherever a single rule is required, and never select a task subset from outer-test performance.

We calculate 95\% percentile confidence intervals from 10,000 diagnosis-stratified bootstrap draws of participants. A draw preserves all available tasks and repeated predictions for each selected person. For a paired contrast, the same draw is applied to both methods. The randomization test exchanges method predictions per participant, jointly across tasks and repetitions, using 10,000 participant-wise swap patterns. It tests the difference between fixed sets of predictions, not a shuffled-label learning pipeline.

For the initial analyses, Holm adjustment covers 36 encoding pairs; two separate 32-contrast task families for isolated signals and triplets against static; six classifier pairs at each endpoint; three SVM fusion-rule pairs; and eight task-specific CNN--SVM contrasts. Each follow-up section defines its additional families. Correction is within each declared family, not across all decisions in the broader research history. The intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous.

Both resampling procedures leave models fixed. Intervals and tests describe conditional uncertainty in these predictions, without the full variability of retraining or selection.

One outcome of these corrections can be stated once. Apart from the contrasts between the LPQ models and the scratch-trained CNN in Section~\ref{sec:classifiers}, no comparison reported in this paper survives its declared Holm family. We therefore give effect sizes with their unadjusted intervals throughout, and report an adjusted $p$ only where its value carries information, instead of restating the outcome of the correction at every comparison.
