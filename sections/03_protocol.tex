\section{\uppercase{Data and Evaluation}}
\label{sec:protocol}

\noindent
The evaluation is organized as three experiments that share the same participant partitions. Experiment~1 (Sections~\ref{sec:descriptor}--\ref{sec:classifiers}) compares the nine encodings of Table~\ref{tab:encoding} under a fixed descriptor and classifier. Experiment~2 (Sections~\ref{sec:nested-followup} and~\ref{sec:controls}) selects the encoding using training participants only and tests controls that displace the signals along each stroke. Experiment~3 (Section~\ref{sec:competitive}) compares minimaps, described by LPQ texture or by pretrained ResNet18 features, with the static trace and with kinematic/pressure attributes on three tasks fixed from prior work.

\subsection{The PaHaW Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}
\noindent
PaHaW includes 37 PD patients (19 men) and 38 healthy participants (20 men), all native Czech speakers writing with their dominant right hand, with the patients recorded under L-DOPA medication \cite{Drotar2016}. They wrote with an inking pen on an empty paper template laid over a Wacom Intuos 4M tablet, which recorded position, pressure and pen status during on-surface and in-air movement at 100 samples per second; the released recordings also contain altitude and azimuth. The dataset has since been used to study which handwritten elements carry Parkinsonian alterations \cite{Casademunt2024}.

The eight tasks progress from drawing to writing. T1 is an Archimedean spiral drawn from the inside out; T2--T4 repeat the cursive letter \textit{l}, the bigram \textit{le} and the trigram \textit{les}; T5--T7 repeat the Czech words \textit{lektorka}, \textit{porovnat} and \textit{nepopadnout}, which can be written without lifting the pen; and T8 is the sentence \textit{Tramvaj dnes u\v{z} nepojede}, which adds in-air transitions between words \cite{Drotar2016}. Our local copy contains 597 recordings: the spiral has 72 (36 PD, 36 H) because three are absent, and every other task has one recording per participant (37 PD, 38 H). We calculate speed from the recorded timestamps rather than an assumed sampling interval; every negative timestamp increment falls outside a contact segment, so Equation~\ref{eq:speed} requires no interpolation.

The participant is the independent observational unit; tasks and alternative renderings do not enlarge the cohort. Mean age is 69.32 years in PD and 62.42 in H. Age and clinical fields are excluded from classifier inputs, but handwriting may still reflect this difference.

\subsection{Partitions, Metrics and Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}
\noindent
We construct stratified folds from one row per participant and propagate the assignments to every task and rendering, so that all tasks from a participant fall in the same outer fold. Five repetitions of five outer folds produce a test prediction for every available record in each repetition. Within each outer training partition, three participant-separated inner folds choose the model hyperparameters; standardization is fitted on the corresponding training portion, never on its validation or test participants, and the selected model is refitted on all outer training participants and evaluated once on the outer test set. Rendering is independent per record, so it learns nothing from other records before splitting. Experiment~1 chooses its leading encoding from these outer-test scores for the classifier comparison, so Experiments~2 and~3 repeat the encoding choice with training participants only.

Macro F1, the unweighted mean of the two per-class F1 scores, gives equal weight to the two diagnoses. For each task and repetition we pool the five outer-test folds and compute this \emph{recording-level} macro F1 once, then average the five repetitions; the unweighted mean over the eight tasks is the endpoint used to rank encodings, and Experiment~3 uses the corresponding mean over T2--T4. The \emph{participant-level} endpoint combines the tasks into one decision per participant by one of three rules: probability averaging over the calibrated PD probabilities, weighted averaging, which favors tasks whose models scored higher on validation participants, and majority voting, with ties assigned to PD. A missing spiral recording is left out of that participant's decision, and because every task shares the participant folds, no model contributing to a participant's decision was trained on that participant. In Experiment~1 the three rules gave similar macro F1 (0.6341, 0.6447 and 0.6488); Experiment~2 chooses the rule within training, and Experiment~3 fixes majority voting before fitting. We also report accuracy, balanced accuracy, PD sensitivity, H specificity and ROC AUC.

We calculate 95\% percentile confidence intervals from 10,000 diagnosis-stratified bootstrap draws of participants, each draw keeping all of a participant's tasks and repeated predictions and a paired contrast applying the same draw to both methods, and the randomization test exchanges method predictions per participant with 10,000 swap patterns, both with the fitted models held fixed. Holm adjustment \cite{Holm1979} is applied within families declared separately for each experiment, and with 75 participants only one difference survives its family (Section~\ref{sec:descriptor}), so we report effect sizes with their 95\% intervals throughout.
