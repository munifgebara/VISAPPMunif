\section{\uppercase{Competitive Baselines on a Fixed Task Subset}}
\label{sec:competitive}

\noindent
The preceding comparisons examine image texture and a CNN trained from scratch. We now broaden the reference methods to include the original movement signals and pretrained visual features. A task set drawn from related work allows us to examine these alternatives under a shorter handwriting protocol, while retaining all eight tasks as a secondary comparison.

\subsection{Task Set and Evaluation Protocol}
\noindent
The primary set comprises repeated \textit{l}, \textit{le} and \textit{les} (T2--T4), the tasks reported in the reference thesis~\cite{Casademunt2023}. We fix this set before fitting the new models. Combining its three tasks by voting is our adaptation; the thesis does not report this ensemble. We evaluate four methods: static LPQ with SVM, LPQ with internal selection among nine encodings, a kinematic/pressure SVM, and a frozen ResNet18 with a trained linear classification head. All eight tasks form a separately specified secondary evaluation.

The methods share the original 25 outer participant partitions and three common inner participant folds. For each task and eligible encoding, mean inner-fold macro F1 selects the classifier parameters. The two image methods that allow encoding selection choose one encoding for the task set by averaging these selected inner scores across its tasks. Candidate order resolves ties, with static first among encodings. Each method makes its own choices using only outer-training participants. Feature standardization is fitted within the corresponding training partition; selected models are then refitted on the complete outer-training set. The three SVM methods retain the same 21-candidate linear/RBF search.

Our primary endpoint is participant-level macro F1 from the fixed majority vote of Section~\ref{sec:fusion} over T2--T4, with no calibration, learned weights or rule selection. Task-mean macro F1 is secondary, because means over three and eight tasks describe different task populations and cannot by themselves establish a benefit from reducing the task set; the paired fusion comparison instead evaluates the same 75 participants, although changing the task set can also change the internally selected encoding. Bootstrap intervals and paired prediction swaps follow Section~\ref{sec:protocol}. Holm adjustment covers the six method pairs separately at each endpoint, and the four within-method contrasts between three-task and eight-task fusion form another family.

\subsection{Kinematics and Pressure}
\noindent
The signal baseline retains quantities removed by image fitting and per-record colour normalization. Its 83 fixed attributes, motivated by earlier kinematic and pressure measurements on PaHaW~\cite{Drotar2016}, comprise 11 acquisition times, contact fractions, stroke counts, path lengths and contact dimensions; 24 contact and in-air stroke duration and length summaries; 36 contact and in-air speed, acceleration and jerk summaries; and 12 contact pressure and absolute pressure-rate summaries. Each distribution contributes its mean, population standard deviation, median, 5th and 95th percentiles, and interquartile range. This is an independently specified descriptor, with no supervised feature screening or claim to reproduce their exact pipeline.

Coordinates and pressure remain in native tablet units; timestamp differences are divided by 1,000 to obtain seconds. Velocity vectors are secants at interval midpoints. Successive vector differences over midpoint time differences yield acceleration and jerk, whose magnitudes are summarized. Derivatives never cross pen lifts or invalid intervals. No smoothing is applied, so quantization can influence higher derivatives.

A pre-fit audit excluded 255 of 1,364,022 intervals: 253 negative increments and two positive jumps exceeding 60s. None occurred within contact. Positive intervals up to 60s are retained, including 145 gaps of 1--26.61s; their velocities describe average movement across the gap. Acquisition time sums valid intervals, dividing each state-transition interval equally between contact and air. Stroke lengths and derivatives exclude transitions. Six zeros represent an absent distribution, recorded separately in diagnostics: 56 recordings lack in-air distributions and 57 lack an in-air jerk distribution. These conventions preserve every recording while making incomplete temporal information explicit.

\subsection{Transfer from a Pretrained CNN}
\noindent
ResNet18~\cite{He2016} supplies 512 features from its global average-pooling layer using the fixed torchvision \texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V1} weights. Images undergo the same foreground crop and square white padding as LPQ, followed by bilinear resizing to $224\times224$ and fixed ImageNet channel normalization. The backbone remains in evaluation mode, with convolutional weights and batch-normalization statistics frozen. There is no augmentation or end-to-end fine-tuning.

A class-balanced, $L_2$-regularized logistic head is fitted to standardized features. Its inner search evaluates seven values, $C\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,100\}$, and selects among the same nine image encodings as LPQ. This comparison therefore concerns complete learning procedures with different feature families and search budgets. It evaluates fixed-feature CNN transfer on PaHaW, without attributing any difference solely to architecture.

\noindent
Training-selected LPQ+SVM has the highest observed participant macro F1, 0.6449 (Table~\ref{tab:competitive}). Its difference from static LPQ is 0.0307, with interval [$-$0.0107, 0.0748], unadjusted $p=0.1815$ and adjusted $p=1.0000$. Its differences from the kinematic and transfer pipelines are also uncertain. All six primary adjusted $p$ values equal one; this does not establish equivalence among the methods.

The selected-encoding SVM has PD sensitivity 0.5892 and H specificity 0.7053. Thus its macro F1 does not imply equally strong performance in both classes. Its vote-fraction AUC is 0.6139, whereas the kinematic model reaches 0.6691 despite a lower thresholded macro F1. Macro F1 evaluates the fixed voting decision; AUC evaluates ranking by the coarse vote fractions.

Kinematics have the highest individual-task mean, 0.6074, although LPQ-selected fusion is numerically higher. An average task score does not encode the coincidence of errors across tasks. We report the two endpoints separately and do not test their numerical difference as an improvement. Neither the six task-mean contrasts nor the primary fusion contrasts supports a method advantage after correction.


\subsection{Does Reducing the Task Set Help?}
\noindent
With all eight tasks, fixed majority fusion reaches macro F1 0.6330 for static LPQ, 0.6166 for selected LPQ, 0.5941 for kinematics and 0.6225 for transfer features. These values use a fixed vote rather than the internally selected fusion rule of Section~\ref{sec:nested-followup}; their differences across sections do not represent contradictory estimates of the same procedure.

Moving to three tasks changes participant macro F1 by $-$0.0188 for static LPQ, +0.0283 for selected LPQ, +0.0338 for kinematics and $-$0.0204 for transfer features. Every interval includes zero and every adjusted $p$ equals one in the four-comparison family; for selected LPQ the interval is [$-$0.0358, 0.0950]. The shorter set is therefore a plausible option for further study, with a numerical gain for two pipelines and losses for two others; its general advantage is unproven.

The transfer method has the highest eight-task mean at 0.5813, compared with 0.5618 for training-selected LPQ. Their difference, 0.0195 [$-$0.0152, 0.0543], is not supported after correction. This result limits the earlier conclusion about the small scratch-trained CNN: it does not justify a general ranking of texture descriptors above pretrained CNN features.
