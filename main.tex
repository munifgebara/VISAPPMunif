\abstract{Digitizing tablets capture handwriting trajectories together with dynamic information such as speed, pressure and pen orientation. We introduce \emph{pseudodynamic minimaps}, a new visual representation that encodes these dynamics along the contact trajectory, preserving handwriting geometry in a compact image. The formulation combines contact-only rendering, percentile-based normalization and circular azimuth centering. We evaluate them on 597 recordings from 75 participants of the PaHaW Parkinson's disease dataset, in three experiments with participant-separated cross-validation: a comparison of static traces, single-signal encodings and RGB combinations of speed, pressure, altitude and azimuth under a Local Phase Quantization descriptor and an SVM; a training-only selection of the encoding, with spatial controls that displace the dynamics along the trajectory; and a comparison with kinematic features and with pretrained ResNet18 features. Training-only selection preferred a dynamic encoding in 24 of 25 training sets, and on a predefined three-task subset the minimap pipeline achieved the highest participant-level macro F1 (0.6449), compared with 0.6143 for static images, 0.6279 for kinematic features and 0.6021 for ResNet18 features. The direction of these effects is consistent, but their magnitude is small and does not survive multiplicity correction on a cohort of this size. We therefore present pseudodynamic minimaps as an explicit way to carry pen dynamics into image-based handwriting analysis, to be tested against closely related encodings and on independent cohorts.}
