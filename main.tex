\keywords{Pseudodynamic Minimaps, Handwriting, Time-Series Imaging, Local Phase Quantization, Parkinson's Disease.}
\abstract{A digitizing tablet records pen speed, pressure and inclination alongside the trajectory it traces. We introduce \emph{pseudodynamic minimaps}, a formulation that renders these signals as colour along a contact-only trajectory, combining within-recording percentile normalization with circular azimuth centring so that a task execution becomes one compact image. Using 597 PaHaW recordings from 75 participants, we evaluate the construction incrementally: static traces, isolated colour signals, and all four three-signal RGB combinations, under a fixed Local Phase Quantization descriptor, SVM search and participant-separated partitions. We then move encoding selection inside training, add spatial controls that displace signals along each stroke, and compare against kinematic/pressure features and frozen ResNet18 transfer features on three tasks fixed from prior work. Training-selected minimaps reach the highest observed participant-level macro F1, 0.6449, against 0.6143 for static images, 0.6279 for kinematics and 0.6021 for transfer features. The gains are task-dependent: reducing eight tasks to three helps two pipelines and lowers two others. No paired contrast survives multiplicity correction. The study therefore contributes an explicit encoding technique and a controlled account of what its performance depends on, rather than a demonstrated advantage.}
